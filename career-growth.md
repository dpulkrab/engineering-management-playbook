# Career Growth and Feedback

Career development works best as a continuous management practice, not an annual event. The goal is to help an engineer build evidence of operating at broader scope while serving real team and business needs.

## Growth Cycle

1. **Understand motivation.** Discuss the work the engineer wants more of, the capabilities they want to build, and constraints affecting them.
2. **Calibrate current scope.** Compare observed behaviors with the expectations for the current and next level.
3. **Select one or two themes.** Avoid a long development backlog. Choose the capabilities that create the most useful next step.
4. **Create real opportunities.** Assign work with meaningful ownership, visibility, and support.
5. **Observe and coach.** Give specific feedback close to the event and adjust the level of support.
6. **Record evidence.** Capture outcomes, complexity, influence, and lessons, not only completed tasks.
7. **Recalibrate.** Review progress and decide whether to deepen, broaden, or change the growth plan.

## Useful Growth Assignments

- Own a bounded service or technical area end to end.
- Lead a design review involving several teams.
- Improve an operational problem and measure the result.
- Mentor a less-experienced engineer with explicit goals.
- Coordinate a release, migration, or incident follow-up.
- Present a technical recommendation to product or senior engineering stakeholders.
- Turn recurring support work into documentation, automation, or a platform capability.

## Feedback Pattern

Effective feedback is:

- **Specific:** describe the observed behavior and context.
- **Impact-oriented:** explain the effect on users, delivery, quality, or colleagues.
- **Actionable:** identify what to repeat or change next time.
- **Dialog-based:** ask for the engineer's perspective and agree on the next step.
- **Timely:** do not store feedback for a formal review.

## Promotion Readiness

Promotion should recognize sustained performance at the next level. Look for repeated evidence of scope, complexity, autonomy, quality, influence, and organizational impact. A manager's job is to create fair access to growth opportunities and make evidence visible, not to promise an outcome controlled by a wider calibration process.
